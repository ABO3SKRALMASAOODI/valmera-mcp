# Outsource weekly video podcast editing from recordings you already have

If your team records two 45-minute video episodes a week, ask providers to quote **eight full episodes and 32 captioned vertical clips per four-week production cycle**. That is 40 video files. Get two episodes into the same pilot review window before committing. One episode tests editorial quality; the second tests whether your team can keep up with approvals while another cut is in revision.

## Run one week with a late correction

1. **Send two representative episodes together.** Include the separate source tracks where you have them, required spelling, an approved example, planned publish dates and one contact who can approve each release.
2. **Approve the episode and map its clips.** Ask for the source intervals of four possible shorts per episode. Record any source intervals the producer or guest approved for the master but excluded from social use.
3. **Request one factual correction.** For example, suppose the guest corrects a number spoken in episode A and also used in clip A-02. Require the editor to identify both affected files, propose an approved alternative cut and tell the publisher which versions to replace. Keep the other approved files in the release packet.
4. **Review the actual downloads.** In the revised master and A-02, listen through the correction, compare the caption wording and check that viewers still get the necessary context. Check the other episode's delivery date as well. A first master arriving on time does not answer whether the full weekly batch can ship.

A useful quote separates first review, correction turnaround and final delivery. Ask how many episodes can be active simultaneously and which deadline applies when a revision arrives while the next episode is in progress.

## Check internal review capacity before agreeing to volume

A worked example with two 40-minute edited masters and eight 45-second clips takes **86 minutes of uninterrupted first-pass playback** at normal speed. If the approver has one hour before the release decision, that single review slot is already 26 minutes short. Comments and rechecks add time. Book staggered review windows or assign separate approvers with one person signing off on the entire packet.
