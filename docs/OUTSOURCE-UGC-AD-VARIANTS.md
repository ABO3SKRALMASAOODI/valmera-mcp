# Outsource UGC ad editing from footage you already have

If you have creator footage and need more paid-social ads each week, ask the editor to quote one approved product demonstration, two alternate openings and two export shapes. That pilot produces **three creative cuts and six MP4s**. Give each vendor the same footage, brief and review deadline so you can compare how much work your team still has to do before launch.

## Give the editor a pilot they can quote

> We have one creator shoot with three recorded openings for the same product demo. Cut one 20-second body and closing from our supplied takes. Make three 25-second versions using the supplied five-second openings. Keep the body, captions after the opening, offer, closing and audio mix consistent. Deliver vertical 9:16 and portrait feed 4:5 MP4s with English captions for each cut. Keep the opening headline away from interface overlays in both formats. Quote a first draft, one consolidated revision and all six final downloads. Confirm the intake cutoff and delivery times in our time zone. Quote the same scope per week if we send four separate shoots.

Use the [six-file delivery sheet](UGC-AD-VARIANT-DELIVERY.csv) to connect the creative IDs to the exported files. Replace the example names with your ad IDs when the files are uploaded. The vertical and 4:5 copies share the same creative ID. If the 4:5 copy needs a different shot or a different caption, record that change before treating the outputs as the same cut.

The pilot assumes that your team has permission to use the supplied footage and audio in its planned paid placements. Tell the editor which claims, price language and testimonial wording have already been approved. Give one reviewer responsibility for consolidating brand and media-buyer notes.

## Put a late change through all six files

After approving the first previews, ask the editor to replace the date on the shared closing card across all six outputs, retaining the three openings and everything in the body. In the replacement downloads, open every file and check the closing wording, captions at the crop edges, audible dialogue and the approved orientation. Mark the earlier files superseded before handing them to the media buyer. If you use both paid platforms, map the ad IDs back to the exact copies that were uploaded.

## Compare the cost using approved cuts

For each pilot, add the vendor's fee, billed extras and your team's review time at its internal hourly cost. Divide by the number of distinct cuts approved in **all** requested formats by the deadline. For example, a hypothetical $300 quote plus an hour at $60 comes to $120 per approved cut if all three pass. If only two pass in both shapes, it comes to $180 per approved cut. Count late and partially delivered cuts separately so you can see what caused the shortfall.

For ongoing work, four shoots at this scope mean **12 cuts and 24 MP4s per week**. Ask how those shoots enter the queue, who owns the final review, and what happens when a launch changes after approval.
