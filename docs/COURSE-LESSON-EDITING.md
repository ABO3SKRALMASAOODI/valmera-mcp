# AI video editing for online courses: keep the lesson teachable

For a recorded course, choose an AI video editor by asking it to complete one real lesson and one revision. The useful result is a lesson a learner can follow, with readable examples, accurate captions and a working delivery path. A short runtime or a polished opening does not establish those things.

## First choose the job

| What you have | What you need | What to test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Instructor footage, screen capture and slides | Edit an existing explanation | Preserve the demonstration, qualifications and correct source selection through a revision. |
| Notes or a script without a recording | Produce a new explanation | Review the generated narration and every diagram against approved material. This is a different trial from editing recorded teaching. |
| A finished lesson | Publish it inside a course | Check the actual player, captions, exercise, access and completion behavior. |
| A course you want to promote | Make a separate teaser | Keep the complete lesson intact; choose a self-contained preview that accurately represents it. |
| A desire to learn editing | Take an editing course | Evaluate the syllabus and exercises, rather than treating the course provider as an editing tool. |

The distinction matters in search. A result about generating an avatar lesson may be useful, but it does not demonstrate preserving a recorded experiment. A result teaching video production is not an editor you can upload your lesson into.

## Write a lesson brief before asking for a faster cut

Name one observable learner outcome. “Understand spreadsheets” is too broad for a single editing decision. “Sort the supplied inventory table by quantity while keeping each item's fields together” gives the review a concrete task.

Record the prerequisites: starting file, relevant application version, account role, vocabulary and any previous lesson. Identify the evidence the video must contain: the starting state, the action, the result and any condition that changes the instruction. For conceptual lessons, identify the explanation, example and counterexample instead of inventing a sequence of clicks.

Keep an instructor-approved reference beside the edit. That could be a slide deck, script, worked solution or answer key. The transcript records what was said; it does not prove the explanation is correct. If the instructor misspoke, mark the passage for correction or a pickup recording. Do not silently replace their meaning with a plausible sentence.

> Inspect this lesson and propose a cut plan. The learner starts with [file/state] and should finish able to [specific task]. Preserve [required examples, qualifications and recovery steps]. Identify useful pauses separately from waiting, and mark incomplete demonstrations or statements needing instructor review. Use original source times. Do not generate missing evidence of a successful result.

## Worked example: an eight-minute lesson with a missing recovery step

The following spreadsheet lesson is **fictional**. It uses one eight-minute recording at normal speed. All ranges are half-open: the end time of one range is the start of the next. There are no overlapping transitions, added intros or speed changes in this arithmetic.

The lesson first shows a deliberately wrong sort, then restores the sample table before demonstrating the correct method. A rushed draft plan retains the wrong result but omits the quiet restoration. That makes the later demonstration start from a state the learner cannot explain.

| Part | Source retained in draft | Draft program range | Instructional purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Setup | 00:00–00:40 | 00:00–00:40 | Identify the sample table and desired result. |
| Explain the fields | 00:50–02:00 | 00:40–01:50 | Show which values belong together in a row. |
| Wrong sort | 02:20–03:20 | 01:50–02:50 | Show the deliberate mistake and its consequence. |
| Correct method | 03:40–05:20 | 02:50–04:30 | Demonstrate the approved procedure and inspect the result. |
| Practice | 05:40–06:40 | 04:30–05:30 | Present a different practice table and an explicit pause instruction. |
| Recap | 07:20–08:00 | 05:30–06:10 | Explain the answer check and next lesson. |

The selected durations total **370 seconds, or 6:10**. Review discovers that source **03:20–03:32** contains the restoration action and a visible check that the original table is back. The eight seconds after that, 03:32–03:40, are idle waiting in this fictional example.

Restore the twelve-second recovery, rather than restoring all twenty seconds or speeding through the action. The revised source selection merges the wrong-sort section and recovery into **02:20–03:32**. The rest of the approved selection stays the same.

| Part affected | Revised source range | Revised program range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wrong sort and recovery | 02:20–03:32 | 01:50–03:02 |
| Correct method | 03:40–05:20 | 03:02–04:42 |
| Practice | 05:40–06:40 | 04:42–05:42 |
| Recap | 07:20–08:00 | 05:42–06:22 |

The revised lesson is **382 seconds, or 6:22**. The extra twelve seconds serve a teaching purpose. Earlier program times remain unchanged; later chapter starts and caption times must be recalculated against the revised edit.

For example, a qualification at source **03:56–04:06** appeared at program **03:06–03:16** in the draft. After restoration it appears at **03:18–03:28**. A note saying “review 03:06” without naming the version now points at the wrong moment.

> Restore source 03:20–03:32, including the table-reset action and visible confirmation, in its original position. Keep the other approved ranges, normal speed, framing and original sound unchanged. Render a new preview, identify its version and report the revised chapter positions. Do not reuse the prior export's caption timings without checking the new program.

Use those numbers only to understand the example. On your own lesson, identify the actual source interval and current project version. For detailed screen-action review, see [screen-recording tutorials](SCREEN-RECORDING-TUTORIALS.md); for corrections after a cut, see [caption revisions](CAPTION-REVISIONS.md).

## Test the parts that an attractive preview can hide

Review the complete downloaded lesson with sound, then try the learner's task using only that lesson and its supplied materials. Record what you had to infer. If you already know the procedure, ask a suitable reviewer who did not make the edit to attempt it; familiarity can conceal a missing instruction.

| Review area | Concrete check | A finding that needs another pass |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meaning | Compare instructions, negations, units and exceptions with the approved reference. | “Do not select only this column” becomes “select only this column.” |
| Visible action | Follow the starting state, action and result for each required step. | The pointer jumps to a completed result with no explanation of how it got there. |
| Reading time | Read the example in the actual player at the intended size. | A full table appears only briefly or a crop hides a relevant header. |
| Practice | Open the correct exercise file and attempt the stated task. | The lesson reveals the answer before inviting a pause, or the file differs from the demonstration. |
| Captions | Check names, technical terms, punctuation, timing and overlaps with important content. | Captions cover the formula bar or change a condition in the instruction. |
| Sound | Listen across cuts, including quiet actions and the ending. | A word is clipped, a level jumps or music masks the explanation. |
| Continuity | Return later and revise one title or instruction in the saved project. | Only the flattened export remains available. |

Do not remove every silence automatically. A pause may allow reading, a calculation or observation of a physical process. If learners need longer than the retained pause, make the pause instruction clear and test that it appears before the solution. A marketing teaser has a different job; use a separate project and review it as an excerpt.

## Deliver a lesson package, not only an MP4

Keep the reviewed video, required caption files, exercise, answer key and lesson metadata together in a versioned handoff. Record which caption file belongs to which video. A caption track from the first cut can drift after a later restoration even when its wording is still correct.

Burned-in captions and player-selectable captions are different outputs. Thinkific's [official caption guide](https://support.thinkific.com/hc/en-us/articles/360030375274-Add-Closed-Captions-to-Your-Videos) describes uploading SRT files and selecting captions through the player's CC control. It also documents plan-dependent automatic captioning. Confirm your destination's current requirements and obtain the needed files; do not assume that visible words in a video constitute a separate subtitle track.

Interactivity is another separate delivery requirement. TechSmith's [Camtasia export guidance](https://support.techsmith.com/hc/en-us/articles/41262572834829-How-do-I-Make-a-Video-With-Camtasia-Editor) distinguishes ordinary video exports from delivery with Smart Player features and links to SCORM/LMS publishing. If your course requires an embedded quiz or score reporting, test that behavior in the intended learning platform. A playable MP4 alone does not establish functioning quiz results or course completion tracking.

After upload, inspect the processed lesson as a learner: beginning, corrected section, captions, exercise links, ending and next-lesson navigation. Follow the platform's supported preview/test-account route. Record whether you replaced an existing asset or created a new one, and check each lesson that references it. Do not assume that replacing one file updates separate promos, translated versions or downloads.

## Questions to settle before editing a whole course

**Which AI editor is best for my lessons?** Choose a shortlist by input and delivery requirements, then run the same representative lesson, correction and export through each. Record review effort and unresolved problems alongside plan costs. This guide provides a method, not completed comparative scores.

**Can I use one template for all lessons?** Share presentation choices such as caption placement and title structure. Keep cuts and timing specific to each recording. A common intro does not justify identical pauses, crops or highlight selections. Run a pilot before applying a style across the library; see [batch editing](BATCH-VIDEO-EDITING.md).

**Should the lesson be a fixed number of minutes?** Set a target if the course requires one, but review what would be lost to meet it. Separate topics when that creates complete lessons with clear prerequisites. Do not remove the only example, exception or recovery merely to hit a runtime.

**What changes when I correct a published lesson?** Track the video version, caption track, chapter timings, exercise, answer key and every destination using the old material. Review the learner-facing result after replacement. A successful edit inside a project is only one part of that update.
