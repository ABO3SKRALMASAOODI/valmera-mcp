## Run the same brief through each route

Use one short narrated walkthrough and one recording with a long wait and a quiet click. Give both routes the same source files, brief and approval rules. Use demo data your team can share.

> Tighten the waits in these uploaded recordings. Keep the spoken instructions, the click that applies the change and the visible confirmation after it. Add captions, preserve the product's UI labels and provide a landscape MP4 for review. After the first cut, restore one second of the final state without undoing the other cuts.

Open the exported MP4 for the check. Confirm the viewer can identify the clicked control and read the result, the narration makes sense across the join, and the captions leave the control and result visible. If your own recording lacks any key step, record a pickup before using it as a test. [See how a silent click can disappear in a cut](SCREEN-RECORDING-TUTORIALS.md#find-the-missing-click-in-an-actual-screen-recording) or [watch a sample Valmera click-to-export workflow](https://valmera.io/examples/product-demo). The sample export is silent. Use your two narrated recordings to test speech and caption behavior.

| Measure | What to record for each route |
| --- | --- |
| Staff time | Minutes spent preparing footage, giving instructions, reviewing, requesting a revision and checking the download. Add time from each person who takes part. |
| Elapsed delivery time | Time from a complete brief to a downloadable, approved file. Keep this separate from staff time so a long review queue is visible. |
| Cash cost | Software, capacity or usage needed for the month, or the service quote including the versions and revisions you expect. Add any assistant subscription you need specifically for the work. |
| Accepted output | How many scoped deliverables passed review by the deadline. If a master and two variants belong to one bundled deliverable, count them on the same basis for both routes. |

## Work out the cost at your expected volume

Multiply expected staff hours by the hourly cost your team uses for planning. Add monthly cash cost. Divide by the number of approved videos you expect to receive by the deadline. For the first month, include any one-time setup each route requires and show it on its own line so you can compare month two.

Suppose your team spends 30 minutes per approved video in an editor and 10 minutes per approved video with a service. Staff time costs $60 per hour. The editor and usage total $60 per month, and the service quote is $540 for up to 30 approved videos at the same scope.

| Approved videos | In-house editor | Done-for-you service |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 12 | $60 + $360 staff time = $420, or $35 per video | $540 + $120 staff time = $660, or $55 per video |
| 24 | $60 + $720 staff time = $780, or $32.50 per video | $540 + $240 staff time = $780, or $32.50 per video |
| 30 | $60 + $900 staff time = $960, or $32 per video | $540 + $300 staff time = $840, or $28 per video |

The cash-cost difference is $480. Each video saves 20 minutes of staff time, worth $20 at this hourly rate. So the two costs meet at **24 approved videos** under these assumptions. If you need an editor upgrade, extra revision rounds or a service tier that changes with volume, recalculate at that volume. Check whether both routes can meet the deadline before using the cost comparison.
