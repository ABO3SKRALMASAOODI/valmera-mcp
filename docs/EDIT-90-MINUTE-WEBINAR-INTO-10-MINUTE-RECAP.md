# How to turn a 90-minute webinar into one 10-minute recap

Make one chronological MP4 with the useful actions, the evidence they worked and the answer that helps the next viewer. When proof appears without narration, give the editor its exact source range. Put it on the review checklist and verify the downloaded file after every change.

Valmera publishes this guide. The 90-minute timeline below is a worked example you can replace with your own source times. It gives you expected results for a pilot. The separately linked Valmera revision is an actual shorter export.

## Build the cut

Use the clock of the original recording for every source range. Mark the visual proof by watching with sound off. In this example, the report changes from “Processing” to “Export ready” during a silent 12-second passage. Retain the time before the state change so the viewer can see what happened.

| Beat | Source range for first cut | First recap | What must survive |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening | 00:04:20–00:05:00 | 00:00–00:40 | State the problem |
| Context | 00:12:30–00:13:30 | 00:40–01:40 | Who the workflow is for |
| Setup | 00:21:15–00:22:25 | 01:40–02:50 | Prerequisites and input |
| Walkthrough | 00:32:40–00:34:40 | 02:50–04:50 | Show the action and immediate effect |
| Proof | 00:43:10–00:44:55 | 04:50–06:35 | Retain the uninterrupted silent confirmation from 00:44:17 through 00:44:29 |
| Exception | 00:53:20–00:55:05 | 06:35–08:20 | Include the condition and its workaround |
| Qa | 01:09:00–01:10:10 | 08:20–09:30 | Keep the answer with its qualifier |
| Close | 01:22:30–01:23:00 | 09:30–10:00 | End on the next action |

The first and revised cuts both run 10:00. The example assumes no added gaps, overlaps, titles or end cards. If your deliverable needs those elements, budget their time explicitly.

> Edit my existing 90-minute webinar into one 10-minute landscape MP4. Keep the eight supplied source ranges in order. Preserve the whole silent demonstration from 00:44:17 through 00:44:29, including the completed screen state and the preceding action. Keep the original spoken qualification in the Q&A. Give me a reviewable first cut and tell me where the silent range appears before I approve export.

## Revise the opening and keep the result intact

> Shorten the opening by keeping 00:04:20–00:04:50 of the source. Start the closing excerpt ten seconds earlier, at 01:22:20, and still end it at 01:23:00. Keep every other source range as approved. I need one 10-minute MP4. Give me the new seek times for the demo and Q&A.

In the first cut, the silent demo plays at **05:57–06:09** and the Q&A starts at **08:20**. After the revision, they play at **05:47–05:59** and **08:10**. The closing beat begins at **09:20** and the output still ends at **10:00**. Those timestamps change because the two ten-second adjustments occur on opposite sides of the demo.

## Approve one downloadable file

1. Check the file plays at the start, at the silent demo and through the ending. Confirm it contains a single 10-minute program. If a player displays rounded seconds, inspect the technical duration too.
2. Play the complete 12-second proof with the audio muted. Check that the action and confirmation are readable at the size your audience will watch. Ask someone who missed the webinar to say what changed.
3. Hear the Q&A and its qualifier together. If captions are delivered separately, load them with the final MP4 and seek to the Q&A at 08:10. Check caption timing again at the end.
4. Log time to first review, time spent correcting the cut and time to get an approved MP4. Run the same pilot on a second recording before committing to a weekly tool.
