## Quote the files the league will use

For example, four matches need four horizontal team recaps, four vertical social cuts, one event recap and one sponsor proof. That's **ten MP4s**. If you also need a clean and branded version of each team recap, the total is **fourteen MP4s**. Separate caption files, alternate sponsor graphics and a second language add work. Write out the variants before comparing quotes.

Give each provider the [tournament delivery sheet](TOURNAMENT-HIGHLIGHT-DELIVERY.csv), your actual publish dates and time zone, and these details:

- **Source arrivals.** List each game, cameras or folders, source length, who sends the footage and when it will actually be available. Head-to-head games may require two different team or sponsor versions from the same recording.
- **First review and final delivery.** Specify both times. Name the person who can approve a replacement play, crop or on-screen name. Decide what to publish first if a camera upload arrives late.
- **Review workload.** Ask who logs the key plays, who picks the usable takes, and who checks every crop. Compare quotes by approved MP4 and log your team's own review minutes.
- **Separate audiences.** Keep internal game review, public social and sponsor proof as distinct outputs. In the delivery sheet, mark footage that needs confirmation before public use.

## Give each provider a two-output pilot

> From Match 2, keep the complete buildup and scoring play at the source times in the brief. Deliver one 16:9 recap and one 9:16 clip from that sequence. Preserve enough lead-in to see who receives the pass. If portrait framing would crop a required player or the ball, show a wider treatment before final delivery. Send the review files with source times. After review, replace only the flagged angle and send the approved MP4s under new filenames. Leave publishing to our team.

Check the same three beats in each file. Use the source clock to find them and the export clock to verify their position.

| Beat | What the person approving the MP4 should see |
| --- | --- |
| The pass develops | The passing player and the direction of the ball are clear before the camera change. |
| The receiving player enters | The incoming player and the ball are visible together long enough to follow the play. |
| The play resolves | The shot includes the result, then moves to the reaction. The reaction does not replace the result. |

Even an accurate trim can fail the portrait check. In a 1920 × 1080 source, a full-height center crop for 9:16 covers only about **608 source pixels** of width. If the ball and receiving player are 820 pixels apart at the deciding frame, no single crop at that scale can fit both. Ask the editor to preview a wider composition with a background or use a different camera angle. Rewatch the motion around that frame before accepting the file.
