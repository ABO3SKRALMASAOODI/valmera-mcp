# Outsource training-video editing with a caption glossary and a revision pilot

Before committing to a monthly batch of training videos, ask the editor to revise three representative lessons against a short glossary. Include a correct spelling, a historical label to preserve and an ordinary phrase that resembles the product name. Request the final MP4s and any required caption files. Open the delivered versions and verify the actual wording and timing against the approved cut.

## Give the provider three distinct cases

In this fictional course, the current product name is **BoardView**. An archived screen still says **Board View**, and an instructor uses lowercase **board view** as an ordinary phrase. The audio supports the intended text in each case.

| Lesson | What to correct | What to preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 01 · Getting started | Caption the current product as **BoardView**. | Keep **Board View** when the instructor reads the archived label. |
| 02 · Review a task | Correct **boardView** to **BoardView**, with exact capitalization. | Keep the approved cue boundaries, including at 9.500–11.400 seconds. |
| 03 · Set up a view | Apply **BoardView** to the marked product reference. | Keep **board view** in the ordinary description. |

The provider should identify the clips and timestamps it changed. If a spoken sentence is actually wrong, record a pickup or approve a separate audio edit before changing the caption. [Valmera caption instructions](https://valmera.io/docs/captions)

## Run the scorecard on the returned delivery

[Download the free Excel scorecard](training-caption-revision-pilot.xlsx). It has six fictional checks across three lessons. The sample shows three passing cues, two to review and one with delivery evidence still pending. One failure is case-sensitive spelling. The other moves both timing boundaries 200 milliseconds later while leaving the displayed word correct.

1. Put the approved word or phrase and cue boundaries next to the delivered values. Use the same program timeline and measure both boundaries in milliseconds.
2. Listen at each flagged cue and inspect the actual video or caption file. If an approved exception appears, check its screen or spoken context before requesting any change.
3. For sidecar captions, run a full comparison of the approved SRT and the revised SRT for each lesson. Review changes outside the sampled cues. For burned-in captions, inspect the affected frames in the exported MP4 and check that their bounding region is still readable. [Free SRT comparison](https://valmera.io/tools/compare-srt-files) · [Video handoff checks](VERIFY-TRAINING-VIDEO-HANDOFF.md)
4. Send a second small revision after the team processes its first notes. Verify that the earlier fixes remain in the new delivery and confirm how the provider would repeat the check on a full weekly batch.

The scorecard compares the wording and boundaries you enter. It does not read the video or subtitle files. Duplicate the workbook for each provider so their deliveries remain traceable.
