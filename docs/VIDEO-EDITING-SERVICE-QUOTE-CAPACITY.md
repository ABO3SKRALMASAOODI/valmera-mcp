# How to compare video editing service costs when several videos are due at once

Get a quote for the same footage and the same delivery deadline from each service. Ask how many requests can be active at once and what happens to that capacity while a cut waits for feedback. Then compare **cost per approved video delivered by the deadline**. Include the buyer’s time for review and the cost of any revisions outside the quote.

That calculation matters for a launch, a training rollout or an agency deadline. A provider can take every request and still finish them one at a time. If you have room in the schedule, a lower-cost queue may meet your needs. With fixed dates, ask the provider to show when each approved file will arrive.

## Worked example with six videos due in six business days

Assume each edit occupies one slot for three business days. The provider sends the first cut on the first day. The customer returns notes on the second, and the corrected version is approved on the third. All six jobs are ready to start on business day 1. The deadline is the end of day 6.

The dollars are illustrative. Each quote covers the same six videos, and the buyer values 15 minutes of review per video at $60 an hour. That adds $90 across the six, including approval work after the deadline.

| Illustrative quote | First group approved | Second group approved | All six approved | Cost per approved video by day 6 | Cost per video once all six are done |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| $600 with one active slot | 1 on day 3 | 1 on day 6 | Day 18 | $345.00 | $115.00 |
| $900 with two active slots | 2 on day 3 | 2 on day 6 | Day 9 | $247.50 | $165.00 |
| $1,320 with three active slots | 3 on day 3 | 3 on day 6 | Day 6 | $235.00 | $235.00 |

In the two-slot example, $990 covers the batch including review, and four files are ready by day 6. Dividing $990 by four gives $247.50 per on-time video. Two files are still late. Mark a quote as infeasible if it misses a required file, even when its per-video price is attractive.

If jobs vary in length or a slot reopens during customer feedback, ask the provider to schedule individual first cuts and revisions. Include internal review capacity. Three simultaneous drafts can still wait on the same approver.

## Send the same quote brief to each provider

> Six videos need approval by the end of business day 6. The footage, brand assets and example are ready on day 1. Include one review round and tell us when the first and revised cuts will arrive for each item. We will reply to draft notes within one business day. List how many edits can be active together, how revisions affect that limit and what extra work would change the quote.

For a pilot, choose two clips with a shared change after first review, such as replacing the same lower third. Record when the source was handed over, when the first drafts arrived, when notes went back and when approved MP4s became available. Use those times to estimate your next batch. Keep rush fees, any separate captions or aspect ratios, and budget for customer review visible in the comparison.
